Stathmin is overexpressed in a variety of assessed human malignancies and is correlated with tumor
progression and poor prognosis. Downregulation of its expression will contribute to optimize therapeutic
outcomes in the treatment of various malignancies. However, the mechanisms of stathmin gene
overexpression are not completely elucidated at present. Early growth response 1 (Egr1) is a transcription
factor that triggers transcription of downstream genes mediating cell growth and angiogenesis upon various
stimulations. Following the previous computational identiﬁcation of a site that was thought to be an Egr1
consensus binding sequence at −85 to −94 region in stathmin gene promoter, we analyzed the role of Egr1
in the regulation of stathmin gene expression in lung cancer cell line A549. The results showed that Egr1
transcription factor bound to the sequence 5′-GCGGGGGCG-3′ within human stathmin gene promoter; and
in reporter gene assays and overexpression experiments, both stathmin gene promoter activity and stathmin
gene expression level were downregulated following endogenous or exogenous expression of Egr1. Using
wild type Egr1 and knockout Egr1 cell lines, we demonstrated that p53 negatively regulates stathmin
expression through Egr1 pathway. In summary, Egr1 is a novel regulator of stathmin expression and p53
mediates the transcriptional repression of stathmin by Egr1 in human lung cancer cells.

1. Introduction
Stathmin is a major cytosolic phosphoprotein that plays a crucial role
in the control of cellular division and proliferation by regulating the
dynamics of the microtubules that make up the mitotic spindle [1–3].
Stathmin is expressed at high levels in a wide variety of human
malignancies including lung cancer, uterine cervix cancer, bladder
cancer, breast cancer, osteosarcoma, showing that the overexpression of
stathmin gene plays an important role in maintenance of malignant
phenotype in human malignancies [4–6]. Our previous studies on
stathmin have shown that inhibition of stathmin expression in
malignant cells can abolish their transformed phenotype [7–9],
however, why and how stathmin is overexpressed in a lot of human
malignancies is not fully understood. One strategy is to investigate the
transcriptional regulation of stathmin gene expression.
Our computer-assisted analysis of the unique proximal promoter
of human stathmin gene, which contains multiple GC-rich regions,
identiﬁed a potential binding site for Egr1 transcription factor at −85
to −94 region. Egr1 preferentially binds to the GC-rich sequence 5′-
GCGGGGGCG-3′ [10,11].
Egr1 (also called Zif268, Krox24, NGFI-A, Tis8) is a zinc-ﬁnger nuclear
phosphoprotein and belongs to the family of cellular immediate early
genes of transcription factor and is rapidly induced by various mitogens,
growth factors, and ionizing radiation [12–14]. Phorbol 12-myristate-13-
acetate (PMA) is a potent mitogen and can strongly upregulate Egr1
biosynthesis [15]. Egr1 is mainly regulated by posttranscriptional
mechanisms, such as phosphorylation [16]. It has been shown that Egr1
stimulates the synthesis of growth and differentiation factor via direct
promoter activation, and in this way, Egr1 acts as an important regulator
of cell growth and differentiation [17–19]. In contrast to this growth
stimulating function, Egr1 binds to the transcription factor c-Jun and
increases the activity of c-Jun to promote cell apoptosis [20]. In our
previous studies, we found that stathmin was overexpressed and Egr1
expression level was low in A549 cells (Fig. 1F and G).
The facts that stathmin gene promoter contains a potential binding
site for Egr1 transcription factor by computational analysis and Egr1 can
also be upregulated by tumor suppressor gene p53, which leads to p53-
dependent apoptosis [21], and p53 is also involved in the negative
regulation of stathmin gene expression [22], while there was no p53

human embryonic kidney cell line 293 (HEK293) were cultured in
Dulbecco′s Modiﬁed Eagle′s medium (Invitrogen, USA) supplemented
with 10% fetal bovine serum and penicillin (100U/ml)/streptomycin
(0.1 mg/ml). All cells were maintained under the atmosphere of 5% CO2
with humidity at 37°C. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs)
from healthy donors were isolated by gradient centrifugation (Sigma,
USA), and were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Invitrogen, USA).
Donors willing to participate were required to complete a written donor
afﬁdavitform,consentingtodonateresiduebloodfor researchpurposes.
For treatment, cells were cultured in the presence of 10 ng/ml phorbol
12-myristate-13-acetate (PMA; Sigma, USA) or 0.5 μg/ml Doxorubicin
(DOX; Sigma, USA).
2.2. UV treatment

USA) which emit UVB (290–320 nm; 75–80% of the total energy) and

experiments. The medium was removed from the dishes and the cells
were rinsed twice with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at 37°C,
drained, and irradiated with the indicated doses of UV. Fresh medium
at 37°C was added after the irradiation.

2.7. Transfection of plasmids
Cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 2.0×105 cells per well
respectively and cultured overnight to 80% conﬂuence prior to
transfection. All transfections were performed using LipofectAMINE
Plus (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer's speciﬁcations.
G418 (600 μg/ml, Invitrogen) was used to screen neomycin-resistant
cells. For stable transfection, single colonies were obtained after
selection with G418 for 2 weeks. All colonies used in this experiment
were analyzed using western blotting.
2.8. Luciferase assays
Cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 2.0×105 cells per well
respectively and cultured overnight to 80% conﬂuence prior to
transfection. For luciferase assays, cotransfections were performed
with 1 μg luciferase reporter gene vector (pGL3-stathmin vector or
pGL3-stathmin-Egr1-mut vector) and 1 μg either pcDNA3.1(+) vector
or recombinant pcDNA3.1 vector. The Renilla luciferase vector was also
cotransfected as an internal control to normalize the transfection
efﬁciency. After 36 h of transfection, cells were washed twice with PBS
and exposed to 250 μl of 1× passive lysis buffer. Luciferase activity was
assayed with 20 μl lysis supernatant and 100 μl luciferase assay reagent
using a luminometer. Luciferase activity was measured using Dual-
luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega). Luciferase assays were
performed as triplicates in 3 independent experiments.

3. Results
3.1. Downregulation of stathmin by DNA-damaging agents or stress is
Egr1 dependent
It has been demonstrated that Doxorubicin (DOX), as a topoi-
somerase II inhibitor, is able to induce double-strand DNA break and
consequently activates Egr1, which results in the induction of Egr1
target genes expression [23]. To explore the relationship between
stathmin and Egr1, we ﬁrst examined the changes of stathmin gene
mRNA and protein levels in response to Egr1 activation by DOX, PMA
and UV. The results showed that DOX treatment resulted in decreased
expression of stathmin gene on both mRNA and protein level in A549
cells as measured by RT-PCR and western blotting (Fig. 1A and B), and
the same results were obtained in the other two classical Egr1
inducible treatment of PMA and UV, which could also downregulate
the expression of stathmin in mRNA and protein level (Fig. 1C and D).
To further conﬁrm that downregulation of stathmin gene expression
is Egr1-dependent, A549 cells were transfected with pSilencer4.1-
Egr1-siR vector to knock down endogenous Egr1. After 24 h of
transfection, cells were treated with PMA. As shown in Fig. 1E,
reduction of endogenous Egr1 markedly attenuated PMA-induced
decrease of stathmin (Fig. 1E). These data strongly suggest that the
decreased expression of stathmin by DNA-damaging agents or stress
is Egr1 dependent.

3.4. Egr1 binds directly to Egr1 binding site in stathmin gene promoter
To further verify that stathmin gene is an Egr1 target gene, ChIP and
EMSA were performed to examine whether Egr1 directly bound to
Egr1 binding site in stathmin gene promoter. Firstly, we demonstrated
that Egr1 could bind Egr1 binding site in stathmin gene promoter using
ChIP assay in vivo. As shown in Fig. 4A, in A549 cells treated with PMA
and transfected with pcDNA3.1-Egr1 vector expressing wild type Egr1,
the fragment containing Egr1 binding site in stathmin gene promoter
was ampliﬁed by PCR from DNA–protein complexes that were
coimmunoprecipitated using the anti-Egr1 antibody. In contrast, a

4 . Discussion
Stathmin is aberrantly overexpressed in the majority of human
malignancies, but the mechanisms for its overexpression in these
tumors have not been fully determined [4]. Although it has been
shown that E2F and NF-Y can bind to stathmin gene promoter and
regulate stathmin expression, stathmin expression is controlled by
multiple signals involved in cell cycle and the regulation of stathmin is
very complicated [24–26]. Our results demonstrated that Egr1 is a
new regulator of stathmin expression.
Decreased levels of stathmin mRNA and protein occurred not only
after treatment with the DNA-damaging agents or stress, which
activates endogenous Egr1, but also following ectopic expression of
Egr1 in A549 cells. Moreover, knock down of endogenous Egr1 markedly
attenuated PMA-induced decrease of stathmin, indicating that this
effect requires intact Egr1. So we hypothesized that stathmin gene
might also be an Egr1 target gene.
While stathmin has an Egr1 responsive element in its promoter, we
identiﬁed one candidate sequence for Egr1 binding site. Our experiments
demonstrate that the putative Egr1 binding site within stathmin gene
promoter is a functional one in response to wild type Egr1, as shown in
reporter assays. Reporter constructs carrying stathmin gene promoter
region and containing Egr1 binding site, exhibited decreased transcrip-
tional activity upon Egr1 regulation. Using ChIP and EMSA, we
demonstrated that Egr1 binding site form DNA–protein complexes in
A549 cells treated with PMA and transfected with wild type Egr1,
suggesting that Egr1 binding site of stathmin gene promoter can bind to
Egr1 after its activation or DNA damage. Collectively, we provide
compelling evidence that Egr1 directly regulate the expression of
stathmin via Egr1 binding site.
p53 is a tumor suppressor protein that regulates many cellular
processes through gene transcription [27–31]. As shown in previous
reports, wild type p53 can downregulate stathmin transcription [22], and
p53/p73-mediated negative regulation of stathmin may occur mainly
through the induction of p21/CDKN1A gene [32]. But the mechanisms of
transcriptional repression of stathmin by p53 remain unclear at present.
Our study proved that Egr1 can directly regulate the expression of
stathmin. Meanwhile, Egr1 is upregulated by p53, which leads to p53-
dependent apoptosis, and Egr1 is also a transcriptional regulator of p53/
p73 [21]. This led us to question whether there is a relationship between
stathmin and p53 by Egr1. Therefore, we investigate the regulation of
stathmin gene promoter, to reveal the relationship of p53, Egr1 and
stathmin. As shownin Fig. 5, induction of Egr1 following p53 upregulation
markedly decreases protein level of stathmin in A549 cells. In contrast,
p53 upregulation did not change protein level of Egr1 and stathmin in
A549-KE cells. Two other classical p53 inducible treatments (DOX
and UV) also got the same result. Taken together, p53 mediates the
transcriptional repression of stathmin gene promoter by Egr1. Additional
experiments will be needed to understand and identify the correct
interaction of Egr1 and p21 on stathmin regulation.
In summary, two important new ﬁndings are described in this
paper. One is the interdependency on transcription factor Egr1 for the
regulation of stathmin expression. The second ﬁnding is that p53
mediates the transcriptional repression of stathmin by Egr1. These
results will help elucidate the mechanisms by which alterations in
stathmin expression occur in response to signals that promote either
cell cycle arrest or cellular proliferation. Furthermore, they have
implications for the origins and progression of human malignancies.
