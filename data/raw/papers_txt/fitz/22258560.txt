ORIGINAL PAPER
The role of upstream stimulatory factor 1 in the transcriptional
regulation of the human TBX21 promoter mediated
by the T-1514C polymorphism associated with systemic lupus
erythematosus
Junggang Li & Jirong Li & Yi You & Song Chen
Received: 9 November 2011 /Accepted: 23 December 2011 /Published online: 19 January 2012
# Springer-Verlag 2012
Abstract T-bet is a key regulator for the lineage commit-
ment in CD4+ T helper (Th) 1 cells by activating the hall-
mark production of interferon-γ. Previously, two single
nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the TBX21 promoter,
T-1993C and T-1514C, have been shown by statistic studies
to associate with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). The
effect of −1993 SNP on the Yin Yang 1 transcription factor-
mediated promoter activity has been already indicated. This
study aimed to investigate roles of the T-1514C SNP on
TBX21 transcription and its functional effect by luciferase
reporter, electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA), chro-
matin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay, and flow cytomet-
ric analysis of intracellular T-bet, IFN-γ, and IL-4
expression in activated CD4+ T cells. The TBX21 promoter
carrying −1514C possessed significantly lower transcrip-
tional activity than that of −1514T and was markedly down-
regulated by the overexpression of upstream stimulatory
factor 1 (USF-1) when compared with the promoter carrying
−1514T. EMSA indicated that the transcription factor USF-
1 was bound to the −1514C allele probe with the affinity
higher than that to the −1514T allele probe. ChIP assay
suggested that USF-1 bound around −1514 of TBX21 ge-
nomic DNA in vivo in the human T cell line Jurkat with
−1514C/T. The individuals carrying −1514C allele were
determined to have significantly diminished expression of
T-bet and IFN-γ and increased IL-4 production in CD4+ T
cells compared with those of −1514T allele. The findings
demonstrate that the T-1514C polymorphism affects TBX21
gene expression and Th1 cytokine production by binding
USF-1 to the SNP site.

transcription factor T-bet (Mullen et al. 2001). T-bet is a
member of T-box family of transcription factors and is
expressed in Th1 but not Th2 cells (Szabo et al. 2000). Ectopic
expression of T-bet in Th2 polarized cells alters their com-
mitment from Th2 into Th1 type by inducing IFN-γ pro-
duction and suppressing IL-4 and IL-5 production (Szabo
et al. 2000). T-bet has a central role in the pathogenesis of
autoimmune diseases like systemic lupus erythematosus
(SLE) (Lit et al. 2007; Chan et al. 2007) and rheumatoid
arthritis (RA) (Kawashima and Miossec 2005).
The human T-bet gene (TBX21) is located on chromo-
some 17q21.32. The constitutive expression of T-bet in
monocytes and lymphocytes is low (Szabo et al. 2000). T-
bet production is mostly regulated at the transcriptional level
(Yu et al. 2007). Several studies have suggested that the
capacity to produce T-bet is influenced by genetic factors
(Miller and Weinmann 2010; Höhler et al. 2005). According
to the results of a twin study, there are striking inter-
individual differences in T-bet production following mito-
gen stimulation (Miller and Weinmann 2010). It has been
suggested that 93% of the variation in the T-bet production
are genetically determined. Indeed, the high T-bet produc-
tion associated with autoimmune diseases like SLE may be
a genetic risk factor for disease susceptibility (Lit et al.
2007; Chan et al. 2007).
The basis for heritable differences in T-bet production is
not known. The TBX21 gene promoter is polymorphic, and
promoter-reporter studies have identified several positive and
negative regulatory promoter sequences within the 2-kb re-
gion upstream of the transcription start site (Yu et al. 2007; Li
et al. 2011; Shin et al. 2009). Three single nucleotide poly-
morphisms (SNPs) at −1993(C/T), −1514(C/T), −999(G/A)
upstream of the transcription start site have been identified
(Suttner et al. 2009).
Recently, T-1993C and T-1514C polymorphisms in the
human TBX21 promoter have been shown to be associated
with autoimmune diseases. For example, the associations
between SNP haplotypes at −1514 and −1993 and SLE
(You et al. 2010), between T-1993C polymorphism and
autoimmune hepatitis (Chen et al. 2011), and between T-
1514C polymorphism and RA have been reported (Chae et
al. 2009). We previously analyzed the effect of a T-1993C
polymorphism on the function of the TBX21 gene and
found that the occurrence of an additional Yin Yang 1
(YY1) motif in the −1993C allele resulted in higher affinity
to YY1, which subsequently caused lower transactivation
of the promoter (Li et al. 2011). The aim of the current
study was to investigate the functional significance of T-
1514C polymorphism in the TBX21 promoter by observ-
ing its association with TBX21 expression and CD4+ T
lymphocytes activation in response to ex vivo mitogens
stimulation and its direct effect on cis-acting transcription-
al regulation of TBX21.

Cell transfection and reporter assays
For transfection, Jurkat cells were plated on 24-well plates in
1 ml of medium at 2×105/ml. The cells were transiently trans-
fected for 24 h with 0.5 mg of each reporter construct and 20 ng
of pRL-TK Renilla reporter plasmid (Promega) using transfec-
tion reagent FuGene 6 (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim,
Germany), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After
transfection, the cells were washed once in phosphate-
buffered saline solution and cultured in 10 ml of complete
medium at 37°C for 24 h. The cells were then stimulated with
1 μg/ml PHA and 0.5 μg/ml ionomycin for 12 h. The cells were
then lysed with the passive lysis buffer (Promega), and the cell
lysate was analyzed using a Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay
System kit (Promega). The firefly and Renilla luciferase were
measured on GloMax® 20/20 Luminometer (Promega), and the
relative luciferase activity was calculated. Where indicated, co-
transfection with pCMV-USF1 was performed. The ratio be-
tween reporter vector and USF-1 expression vector DNA is 2:1.

Genotyping
A total of 370 healthy individuals (men 258; women 112),
with a median age of 37 (ranging from 21 to 57), were
recruited for this study from the Health Examination Center
of Southwest Hospital, Chongqing, China. They are Han
Chinese and live in Chongqing. The protocol for this study
was approved by the Ethical and Protocol Review Committee
of the Third Military Medical University. An informed con-
sent was obtained from each subject.
We extracted genomic DNA from peripheral blood leuko-
cytes of 5 ml whole blood using salting out method. DNA
samples were diluted to 8 ng/μl and distributed to 96-well
plates (DNA panels), each of which contained 94 samples and
two DNA-free control water. The T-1993C and T-1514C
polymorphisms in the TBX21 gene promoter were genotyped
using PCR–restriction fragment length polymorphism assay
as described previously (Chen et al. 2011).
Assessment of intracellular T-bet and cytokine production
in CD4+ T cells
The experiments were performed in whole blood using BD
FastImmune CD4 Intracellular Cytokine Detection Kit (cat#
340970, BDIS, San Jose, CA, USA) following the manu-
facturer’s instructions. Briefly, 0.5 ml of heparinized whole
blood was stimulated by 2.5 μg/ml PHA and 0.5 μg/ml
ionomycin along with co-stimulatory monoclonal antibodies
ant-CD28/ant-CD49d (final concentration 1 μg/ml). The
samples were vortexed gently, incubated at 37°C for 2 h,
and then 10-μg/ml Brefeldin A (BDIS) were added to each
sample. After incubation for 4 h at 37°C, 2-mM EDTA were
added to each tube, vortexed, and incubated for 15 min at
22°C (RT). Blood samples were then lysed and fixed in
10 ml of 1× FACS Lysing SolutionTM (BDIS) for 10 min
at RT. Cells were washed in PBS and frozen at −70°C in a
freezing medium containing 10% DMSO and 1% BSA in
PBS. Ant-CD28/ant-CD49d monoclonal antibodies were
added alone for unstimulated control of the same subject
in each experiment.
For intracellular staining, frozen cells were thawed rapidly
at 37°C, aliquoted into staining tubes at approximately 5×105
cells per tube, and washed once with cold wash buffer (PBS,
0.5% BSA, 0.1% NaN). Cells were subsequently resuspended
in 0.5 ml of FACSTM Permeabilizing Solution (BDIS) for
10 min at RT. After permeabilization, cells were washed once
and staining was performed for 30 min at RT in the dark using
a titrated mixture of fluorescent conjugated monoclonal anti-
bodies. For a five-color analysis of a CD4+ cytokine response,
the staining antibody cocktail consisted of anti-IFN-γ FITC/
CD69 PE/CD4 PerCP-Cy5.5 (BDIS), anti-T-bet Alexa-Flour
647 (eBioscience, San Diego, CA, USA), and phycoerythrin-
Cy7 (PE-CY7) anti-interleukin-4 (IL-4) (BDIS). Isotype-
matched control antibodies were included to detect non-
specific binding to cells.
Flow cytometric analysis
The samples were analyzed with a five-color flow cytometer
(FACS Arial II flow cytometer, BD) using CellQuest™Pro
Software. At least 10,000 CD4+ Tcells were collected for each
sample. Cells were gated using FSC versus SSC to isolate the
lymphocytes and then SSC versus CD4+ T cells to isolate
CD4+ T cells. Dot plots showed events that were displayed
as cytokine+ versus CD69+ T cells. The specific response of
CD4+ T cells to PHA/ionomycin stimulation is obtained by
subtracting the % positive events in the unstimulated sample
from % positive events in the activated sample and then
subtracts the isotype-corrected response of the unstimulated
sample from that of the activated sample.
Statistical analysis
Allele frequencies for the T-1993C and T-1514C SNPs were
determined by gene counting, and the χ2 test was used to
analyze the Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium. While pairwise
linkage disequilibrium measures were analyzed by using a
linkage disequilibrium analyzer, haplotype frequencies
were estimated and calculated using the PHASE package.
Student’st tests were used to analyze the luciferase activities
and the results of intracellular T-bet, IFN-γ, or IL-4 staining
by fluorescent cytometry. A P value of less than 0.05 was
considered significant. Statistical analysis was performed
using SPSS software (version 13.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago,
IL, USA).
Results
The T-1993C and T-1514C SNPs affect the transcription
activity of human TBX21 promoter
To evaluate the effects of SNPs at −1993 and −1514 in the
TBX21 gene on transcription activity, we prepared four report-
er plasmids carrying the luciferase gene under control of the
human TBX21 promoter −2200/−200 region. These four plas-
mids possess sequence variations at −1993 and −1514 of the
TBX21 promoter as shown in Fig. 1b. Each of these reporter
plasmids was transfected into the CD4+ human T lymphoblast
cell line Jurkat. The −1993T/−1514T promoter exhibited the
highest promoter activity, whereas the −1993C/−1514C pro-
moter showed the lowest activity (Fig. 1b). The other two
promoters, −1993C/−1514T and −1993T/−1514C, resulted in
intermediate strength activity. Thus, the −1993T and −1514T
alleles result greater promoter activities than the −1993C
and −1514C alleles, respectively. The effect of the T-1993C

SNP on promoter activity coincides well with our previous
observation, although the role of T-1514C SNP was not dis-
cussed in that study. The comparable activity of the −1993C/
−1514T and −1993T/−1514C promoters suggests that each of
the SNPs at −1993 and −1514 contributes equally to promoter
activity in vitro.
The above-mentioned results suggested that nucleotide
C at −1514 was critical for showing decreased transcription
activity of the TBX21 promoter. Interestingly, in the pro-
moter with −1514C, an additional E-box-binding site core
motif (5′-CACGTG) is present at −1516/−1511 (Fig. 1a).
To confirm the involvement of USF-1 in TBX21 promoter
activity, we cotransfected a USF-1 expression vector with
luciferase expression vectors driven by the TBX21 promot-
er constructs truncated at position −1761. As shown in
Fig. 2, the luciferase activity derived from −1514C promot-
er was 4-fold lower in the presence of overproduced USF-1
and that from −1514T promoter decreased about only 2-
fold. Thus, −1514C promoter responded to USF-1 better
than −1514T promoter.
Identification of a nuclear protein binding at T-1514C locus
YY1-mediated differential transactivation via T-1993C has
been indicated in a previous study (Lit et al. 2007). Similarly,
the significant effect of the SNP at -1514 on promoter activity
suggests that a different transcription factor might recognize
each SNP. Using an online transcription factor search program
TFSEARCH, we found a USF-1- and a C/EBP-responsive
element at −1514 locus. We then performed EMSA with
nuclear extracts from THP-1 and Jurkat cells using double-
stranded oligonucleotides with T-1514C SNP as the probe. As
demonstrated in Fig. 3a, nuclear extracts from human T cell
line Jurkat contained one specific, slowly migrating complex
(complex 1) that bound both the C and the T allele and was
cross-competed by excess unlabeled −1514C and −1514T
oligonucleotides (Fig. 3a, lanes 1–6), and binding of protein
to the −1514C probe was always more efficient than that to
the −1514T probe. A different profile with several different
bands was observed using the extracts from human monocytic
cell line THP-1 (Fig. 3b). EMSAs were repeated with nuclear
extract from Jurkat cells using the USF-1 or C/EBP consensus
oligonucleotides (Fig. 3a, lanes 7–10). The higher molecular
weight complex contained only USF-1 because formation of
the complex was abrogated by excess unlabeled USF-1 con-
sensus oligonucleotides, but not by C/EBP consensus sequen-
ces. Furthermore, the band was specifically eliminated by an
anti-USF-1 antibody, but not by a control IgG (rabbit poly-
clonal C/EBPα) (Fig. 3c, lanes 2–3 and 6–7). A prominent
middle band (complex 2) seen in the EMSA should be con-
sidered to be the USF-1 degradation products because the
shifted band was eliminated by competition with excess
unlabeled USF-1 consensus probe (Fig. 3a (lanes 3–6),
c (lanes 4 and 5)).
To determine whether the USF-1 protein binds the promot-
er region of the TBX21 gene with the −1514C allele in vivo,
we used the ChIP assay. The human T cell line Jurkat, which
was confirmed to be T/C heterozygous at −1514 by sequenc-
ing analysis, was used for this ChIP assay. As shown in
Fig. 3d, USF-1 binding to the −1514 region of the TBX21
promoter was detected in the T cell line (Fig. 3d, lane 3). To
confirm the specificity of antibody immunoprecipitation, a
rabbit IgG was used as a control. This protein does not interact
with this region of the TBX21 promoter as determined by
EMSA, and the antibody was not able to immunoprecipitate a
DNA fragment containing the −1514 residue (Fig. 3d, lane 2).

T-bet production by stimulated CD4+ T cells in association
with the TBX21 −1514 SNPs
To analyze the effect of T-1514C SNP on T-bet produc-
tion, we compared the percentage of T-bet-producing
CD4+ cells among −1514TT (n08) and −1514TC geno-
typic (n06) individuals. As seen in Fig. 4a, PHA/iono-
mycin stimulation of the −1514TT-genotypic T cells after
priming with anti-CD28 plus anti-CD46d mAb resulted
in a significant increase in percentage of T-bet-producing
CD4+ cells compared with those carrying the −1514TC geno-
type (−1993TT/−1514TT+−1993TC/−1514TT versus
−1993TC/−1514TC+−1993CC/−1514TC, 5.1% versus 3.1%,
P00.004). These findings suggest that CD4+ cells carrying
−1514 T allele express higher amount of T-bet production than
those carrying −1514 C allele.
Figure 4b, c shows the composite cytokine profiles of
CD4+ T cells obtained from cultures primed with anti-
CD28 and anti-CD46d mAb in the absence or presence of
PHA/ionomycin. As seen in Fig. 4b, the −1514TT-geno-
typic T cells resulted in a significant increase in percentage
of IFN-γ-producing CD4+ cells compared with those carry-
ing the −1514TC genotype (−1993TT/−1514TT+−1993TC/
−1514TT versus −1993TC/−1514TC+−1993CC/−1514TC,
4.1 versus 2.2%, P00.002). In contrast, CD4+ cells carrying
the −1514TC genotype showed a trend toward an increase in the
percentages of IL-4-secreting cells as compared with those
carrying −1514TT genotype (Fig. 4c). These findings suggest
that individuals with the −1514T allele have a more prominent
proinflammatory cytokine profile, while subjects with the
−1514 C allele have a prominent Th2 cytokine profile.
Discussion
The T-box transcription factor T-bet is a member of the
T-box transcription factor family and has been shown to
be a positive “master” transcriptional regulator of IFN-γ
production in CD4+ T cells (Szabo et al. 2000), NK
cells (Townsend et al. 2004), and effector CD8+ T cells
(Sullivan et al. 2003) and important for the commitment
of CD4+ T cells to Th1 development (Mullen et al.
2001). T-bet has also recently been shown to regulate
murine IFN-γ production by B effector cells (Harris et
al. 2005). In addition, T-bet is elective transducer of IFN-
γ-mediated IgG2a class switching in B cells (Peng et al.
2002) and likely to contribute significantly to the impor-
tance of this regulation in the pathogenesis of humorally
mediated autoimmunity. Generally, enforced expression
of T-bet is associated with increased susceptibility and
severity to organ-specific and systemic autoimmunity
(Lazarevic and Glimcher 2011). Relatively smaller in-
crease in expression of T-bet contributes to the break-
down of self-tolerance (Lazarevic and Glimcher 2011).
Recent data suggest that increased expression of T-bet is
correlated with SLE and especially with lupus disease
activity. Peripheral blood monocytes of SLE patients pro-
duced significantly higher levels of T-bet than that of healthy
controls (Lit et al. 2007). T-bet expression of urinary cells was
significantly higher in SLE patients with active nephritis than
those with inactive disease (Chan et al. 2007). Cytokine assay
of peripheral blood suggests that SLE is a Th2-mediated
disease at the early stage, but the Th1 pathway may become
predominant with the progression of disease and development
of active nephritis (Miyake et al. 2011). An imbalance in the
Th1/Th2 cytokine ratio is thought to contribute to autoim-
mune responses (Chan et al. 2007). These observations call
for a detailed investigation of mechanisms involved in regu-
lation of the TBX21 gene.
Acquired and genetic factors regulate the expression of T-
bet and influence its regulatory potential. Data from several
groups demonstrated differential modulation of T-bet expres-
sion in human monocytes and lymphocytes by cytokines
suggesting that alterations in cytokine levels common during
inflammatory and autoimmune reactions could cause
changes in T-bet expression (Jacobson et al. 1995; Peng et
al. 2002; Yu et al. 2006; Takeda et al. 2003). A role for
hormones in the regulation of TBX21 gene expression was
revealed when estrogen was found to promote T-bet expres-
sion (Karpuzoglu et al. 2007), suggesting a role for sex hor-
mones in the onset and progression of autoimmune diseases.

and T-1514C SNPs is associated with susceptibility to
SLE in a Chinese population. We have previously indi-

activity via different binding affinity to YY1, associates
with autoimmune hepatitis in allele frequency and with
T-bet expression levels, without considering the T-1514C
SNP (Chen et al. 2011). In the present study, we found that
promoter activities of −1993C/−1514T and −1993T/−1514C
are intermediate between those of −1993T/−1514T and
−1993C/−1514C (Fig. 1), suggesting that the effect of both
SNPs on TBX21 promoter activity is almost equal in a basic
molecular analysis. Furthermore, Chae et al. (2009) reported

In electrophoretic mobility shift assays, we were able to
show a stronger binding affinity of the −1514C allele to
Jurkat nuclear factors in comparison with the −1514T al-
lele. Moreover, we characterized this promoter region as an
E-box-binding site. Transcription factors of the E-box fam-
ily are key regulators of genes involved in the immune
response and cellular proliferation. The −1514C allele
matches the CACGTG E-box-binding site core motif,
whereas the −1514T allele differs. The oligonucleotide
encompassing promoter position −1514 contains a C/
EBP-consensus motif a few bases downstream of the SNP,
which also might bind C/EBP transcription factors. How-
ever, two specific complexes were not shown to be an
integral part of C/EBP, based on competition with the C/
EBP consensus sequence and supershift assay. Therefore,
the C/EBP consensus sequence does not bind transcriptions
factors. The specific transcription factor was further char-
acterized as USF-1, an E-box family member expressed in
peripheral T cells (Cippitelli et al. 1996). The binding of
USF-1 to TBX21 promoter was detected in the ChIP assay
using the Jurkat cell line carrying C/T heterozygous
at −1514. This supports that USF-1 bind around −1514 of
TBX21 genomic DNA within living cells. USF-1 has been
shown to either directly or indirectly regulate the expres-
sion of MHC genes and cytokine expression (Howcroft et
al. 1999; Zhong et al. 1999; Weigert et al. 2004), such as
TGF-β1. Our results show that the −1514C allele confers
optimal binding affinity for the transcription factor USF-1
which inhibits gene expression. Therefore, we suggest
USF-1 act as a repressor of TBX21 transcription in Jurkat
cells. Negative regulatory function of USF-1 has been de-
scribed in the regulation of other genes (Cippitelli et al.
1996). Whether repression of TBX21 expression is exerted
by USF-1 alone or by interactions with other transcription
factors remains unclear.
We observed a lower level of IFN-γ production from
CD4+ T cells in the TBX21–1514TC-genotypic carriers as
compared with −1514TT-genotypic individuals, whereas the
opposite changes in IL-4 production from CD4+ T cells were
found in the −1514TC-genotypic versus −1514TT-genotypic
carriers. The potential influence of TBX21 polymorphisms in
the Th1/Th2 cytokine balance observed in our study may also
be of importance in lupus nephritis, a disease in which there is
an increase in the Th1 pathway (Miyake et al. 2011). This
analysis will be performed using larger population including
SLE patients with enough number of rare alleles.
In summary, we demonstrated preferential binding of USF-
1 to the TBX21 gene −1514C allele resulting in lower tran-
scriptional activity. The differences in transcriptional activity
between −1514T and −1514C alleles may potentially be of
some biological importance in CD4+ T cells in which the
potential effect of 1514T>C variant on the proximal promoter
would not be easily masked by potent influences of T-1993C
polymorphism. The associations between TBX21 polymor-
phism and IFN-γ production in CD4+ T cells and/or SLE
may result from haplotypic interplay between functional
T-1993C and T-1514C polymorphisms. We also think that it
is necessary to find polymorphism in other loci of the genome
and analyze its association with T-1993C and T-1514C poly-
morphisms for further elucidation of genomic background for
SLE.
